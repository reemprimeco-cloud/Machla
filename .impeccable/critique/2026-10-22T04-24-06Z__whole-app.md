## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Largely authored for Machla specifically, not category-interchangeable. The two-layer color discipline, the icon-fallback chain, the DB-enforced frozen-request model, and the sender/receipt touches on both sides of a send are all deliberate, product-specific decisions a generic grocery-list skin wouldn't bother with.

**Deterministic scan**: `impeccable detect --json app components` — exit 0, 18 advisory findings, all `design-system-color`/`design-system-font-size`.
- 2 false positives, both already identified and justified in DESIGN.md: the `.hl-gradient-cta::after` 12%/20% black veil (documented AA-contrast fix, not an undocumented color) and the non-Latin `.hl-label` 15px script-bump (documented Script-Bump Rule).
- 15 real, unexplained off-ramp font sizes not covered by any named token rule — concentrated in `HouseholdDashboard.tsx` (7), `ListChecklist.tsx` (3), `WorkerHome.tsx` (2), plus one each in `CategoryGrid.tsx` and `welcome/page.tsx`. **Browser evidence**: `/login`, `/welcome`, `/tips` scanned clean at mobile and desktop widths (live-DOM scan, pre-restart). `/home` and `/worker` require auth and were not reachable — no findings fabricated for them.

## Overall Impression

The design system itself is well-built and consistently applied — this is not a weak implementation. The real finding here is structural, not visual: **PRODUCT.md describes a different product than the one that's shipped.** PRODUCT.md says the household asks and the worker shops/marks items; the actual code (`assert_can_work_list` in `supabase/migrations/20260809190000_phase7_household_lists.sql`, which explicitly rejects a worker-role caller trying to mark purchase status) has it reversed: the worker browses and builds the request ("What do you need?"), the household physically shops and checks items off. Every piece of shipped copy agrees with the code, not the doc — so this isn't a half-built feature, it's a confidently-shipped direction with a stale spec. Once that's resolved, the single biggest real product gap is that the worker — who authored the request — has no way to see which specific item wasn't bought, only a percentage, which cuts directly against the "one source of truth between two people" principle the product is supposed to guarantee.

## What's Working

1. **The two-layer color system is enforced, not just documented** (`app/globals.css`) — a code comment exists specifically warning against the common mistake of using a raw brand hue as text color, and every component reviewed respects the split.
2. **Delete-account in `SettingsScreen.tsx`** is the best-handled high-stakes moment in the app: two-step inline confirm (correctly avoiding `window.confirm`, which silently never appears in the iOS WKWebView shell), sharper owner-specific warning copy, a documented rationale. It's the template the send-list flow should have gotten and didn't.
3. **`groupEntries()` shared between `ListReview.tsx` and `ListChecklist.tsx`** is the actual technical guarantee behind "one source of truth" — enforced at the data layer, genuinely hard for a competitor to casually copy.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] PRODUCT.md describes the opposite of the shipped worker/household role split**
- **Why it matters**: the product's own foundational doc — written this session, from your own answers — says the household asks and the worker shops; the shipped, DB-enforced direction is the reverse (worker requests, household shops). Every future design/product decision made from PRODUCT.md until this is resolved will be reasoning about the wrong product.
- **Fix**: confirm which direction is actually intended, then correct PRODUCT.md (or build the direction it currently describes).
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable document`

**[P0] No account-deletion path exists for the worker role**
- **What**: `deleteAccountAction` only renders in `components/household/SettingsScreen.tsx`. A worker account can never reach `/home/settings` (`requireHouseholdAccess` redirects workers to `/worker`), and the worker's own account surface (`AccountActions.tsx`) has logout/language/privacy/feedback — no delete.
- **Why it matters**: Apple Guideline 5.1.1(v) requires in-app account deletion for every account type. This is a plausible App Store compliance gap, and it also contradicts PRODUCT.md's own principle #2 ("when a tradeoff exists, resolve it toward the worker") — she got the less-complete account surface.
- **Fix**: add a delete-account affordance to the worker's account screen, reusing the same two-step confirm pattern already proven in `SettingsScreen.tsx`.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

**[P0] The worker who built the request has no itemized visibility into what happened to it**
- **What**: `WorkerHistory.tsx` shows only an aggregate "X of Y bought" per list. There is no `/worker/lists/[id]` detail route — the team already knows this (`NotificationsScreen.tsx` has a comment: "A worker has no /home/lists/{id} route to reach").
- **Why it matters**: this directly undermines Principle #1, "one source of truth between two people." The household sees exactly which item is marked unavailable and why; the person who asked for it only sees a number.
- **Fix**: a read-only itemized view for the worker at a new `/worker/lists/[id]` route, reusing `ListChecklist`'s row rendering in non-interactive mode.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable distill`

**[P1] Sending a list — the product's single highest-stakes moment — has no confirmation and no delight**
- **What**: `ListReview.tsx`'s `send()` fires immediately with zero confirm step, and `SentConfirmation.tsx` renders a static checkmark with no entrance motion.
- **Why it matters**: every other irreversible action in the app (delete account, delete list) got a deliberate two-step confirm in the 2026-10-21 polish pass; sending a list — the entire reason the product exists — was skipped. The product's one true emotional peak currently under-delivers.
- **Fix**: no blocking dialog needed (would add friction to the common case), but add brief reassurance copy near Send ("once sent, your home sees exactly this — you can still add more after") and a short scale/draw-in animation on `SentConfirmation` mount.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable delight`

**[P2] Tap feedback is inconsistent on the most-used controls, and two feedback loops under-animate**
- **What**: `QuantityStepper.tsx`'s `StepButton` has an `active:bg-primary-hover` color shift but no `active:scale`, unlike `GlassIconButton`/`PrimaryButton`/`Checkbox` which all use `active:scale-95/98`. `FavoriteButton.tsx`'s heart toggle has no press/pulse feedback at all. `PrimaryPill`'s basket count (the worker's primary "my tap worked" signal) changes instantly with no animation. `app/tips/page.tsx` swaps slide content instantly while its own pagination dot is animated — an internal inconsistency.
- **Why it matters**: for a low-literacy, one-handed, in-aisle-equivalent audience, tactile confirmation matters more than usual, and these are exactly the most-tapped controls in the core loop.
- **Fix**: add `active:scale-90 transition-transform duration-150 ease-hl` to `StepButton`; a brief pulse to `FavoriteButton` on toggle; a scale-bump to the basket pill on count change; match the tips carousel's content transition to its dot's `ease-hl` timing. This is a targeted set of additions, not a general motion pass — the rest of the app (glass surfaces, grids, the gradient CTA) is correctly restrained and should stay that way.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable animate`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-timer, maps to the worker)**: onboarding sets expectations correctly ("What do you need?"), but once a list is sent her only feedback is a percentage — no way to learn what specifically wasn't bought, right when a first-timer most needs reassurance the system worked.

**Casey (distracted/mobile)**: optimistic updates (`useOptimistic` throughout) are a real asset for a flaky connection. Gap: `StepButton`'s missing scale feedback makes it easy to miss whether a distracted tap registered, or double-tap.

**Sam (accessibility)**: strong baseline (48px targets, `aria-live`, `role="alert"`, documented focus-ring invariant). `FavoriteButton` and `StepButton` read like they were added after the primitives were audited for this and didn't get the same pass — worth confirming their focus-visible states directly.
